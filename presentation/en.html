An ordinary day, run from a notebook.

A dairy farm generates dozens of records every day. Written by hand, they get lost, contradict each other, and end up costing milk, calves and money.

  1. 5:30 am

    Morning milking

    Each cow's litres are scribbled on a scrap of paper, when someone remembers. By evening, nobody can find the exact figure.

    Hazel … ? (forgot)
  2. 7:00 am

    The milk collector stops by

    He says 46 litres; the herdsman remembers 52. With no shared written record there is no way to check, and the end-of-month payment depends on it.

    Collector: 46 52 ?
  3. 11:00 am

    Caramel is in heat… or is she?

    The date of her last breeding is somewhere in the notebook. A missed heat means three weeks lost before the next one, and a calf born later.

    Bred on the 3rd or 13th?
  4. 3:00 pm

    Star gets treated

    After a treatment, her milk must be withheld for several days. Who will remember tomorrow, and until which date?

    Discard milk until … ?
  5. 7:00 pm

    The owner calls from town

    “How many litres today? How many cows in calf? When is Daisy due?” Everyone counts on their fingers from an uncertain date.

    Daisy calving: late May?
  6. Month end

    The accounts, and the bloodlines

    Milk sales, feed, the herdsman's wages: the numbers are everywhere and nowhere. And over the years, nobody remembers which dam or which bull each heifer came from.

    Profit: ???

Your questions.

Another question? Email us at gassimbe@gmail.com.

My herdsman isn't comfortable with apps. Can he use it?

Yes, that is what the LITE version is for: big buttons and pictures, milking counted in cans, instructions read out loud, and sign-in with a simple code, with no email address or complicated password.

Can the herdsman see my finances?

No, unless you decide so. Every team member has a role (owner, manager, employee, vet) that sets what they can see and change.

I own several farms. Do I need several accounts?

No. One account handles several farms, and you switch between them in a single tap.

How is the calving date calculated?

From the last natural service or insemination, plus the gestation length for the cow's breed: 280 days for a Holstein, 285 for a Montbéliarde, Normande or Simmental, 290 for a Brown Swiss.

Do I need an internet connection?

Yes: your data is stored online, so the owner sees right away what the herdsman entered, and nothing is lost if a phone is broken or stolen.

Is my data protected?

Traffic is encrypted, passwords are never stored in plain text, and access is restricted by account and by role. Your data is never sold or used for advertising. Details are in our privacy policy (in French).
